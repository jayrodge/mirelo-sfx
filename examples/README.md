# Example clips

## Artemis liftoff (quickstart)

`artemis-liftoff.mp4` is an eight-second silent, real-world video of Artemis I
lifting off. The bright exhaust plume and rising rocket give the sound prompt
a clear visual cue. `artemis-liftoff.jpg` is a frame from the edited clip.

**Video source:** NASA/Sam Lott, *Artemis I Launch (Press Site)*,
November 16, 2022.

- [NASA source record](https://images.nasa.gov/details/Artemis%20I%20Launch%202022%20CU%20tracking%20from%20Press%20Site_compressed)
- [Original MP4](https://images-assets.nasa.gov/video/Artemis%20I%20Launch%202022%20CU%20tracking%20from%20Press%20Site_compressed/Artemis%20I%20Launch%202022%20CU%20tracking%20from%20Press%20Site_compressed~orig.mp4)
- [NASA media-use guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- [Public-domain source record on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Artemis_I_Launch_(Artemis_I_Launch_2022_CU_tracking_from_Press_Site_compressed).webm)

NASA-created footage is generally not subject to copyright in the United
States. This example uses it for an informational developer demo under the
linked media-use guidelines. NASA/Sam Lott is credited for the source footage;
NASA has not reviewed or endorsed this integration.

**Edits:** retained source time 00:55.000–01:03.000, removed all original audio
and metadata, resized to 1280×720, converted to 30 fps and H.264. No source
audio is included. The JPEG is a frame at three seconds into the edited clip.

Suggested sound prompt:

> Deep rocket-engine rumble builds with the bright exhaust plume, swelling
> into a powerful sustained roar as the rocket lifts off. No speech, countdown,
> music, or extra explosions.

Any replacement audio produced with this skill is **AI-generated by Mirelo**.
It is a creative sound design demonstration, not the actual launch recording
or a scientifically accurate reconstruction. When sharing or presenting a
generated result, retain the video source credit and label the replacement
audio as generated by Mirelo.

This clip has no impact-timing sidecar, so the result omits `sync.ok` and
`expected_impacts_s`. The tool has no expected impact times to compare
against. Review the build and liftoff timing by watching and listening together.

To reproduce the silent clip from a downloaded original:

```bash
ffmpeg -ss 55 -i artemis-launch-original.mp4 -frames:v 240 \
  -map 0:v:0 -an -map_metadata -1 -vf 'scale=1280:720,fps=30' \
  -c:v libx264 -crf 21 -preset medium -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -movflags +faststart artemis-liftoff.mp4
```

## Bouncing ball (sync fixture)

`silent.mp4` is the original eight-second synthetic bouncing-ball clip.
`silent.json` records expected impacts at 2.0 and 5.0 seconds. The automated
tests and September 30 qualification runs use this pair. Keep the fixture
for repeatable onset checks; the expected times are not detected audio.
